# Conclusion {#sec:conclusion}

## Summary

This project set out to build and rigorously evaluate a hybrid recommendation engine that combines collaborative filtering, feature-based content matching, and learning-outcome signals on the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset. It uses the CM3005 Data Science template: an investigation of a specific question against a real dataset, delivered as a reproducible pipeline with quantitative evaluation. Seven recommenders — Random, Popularity, truncated-SVD, Alternating Least Squares, content-based, weighted Hybrid, and a per-user Gated Hybrid — sit behind a common interface and are scored under identical conditions. The evaluation reports six metrics on a primary temporal split, five-fold temporal cross-validation, 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, paired-*t* tests with Bonferroni adjustment across the five metrics, an ablation study, cold-start stratification, a fairness audit across gender, IMD band, and disability, and hyperparameter sensitivity across four axes. The Gated Hybrid is the leading model on Precision@10, NDCG@10, and outcome-weighted Precision@10 on the primary split, and its lead survives cross-validation. The relative gain over the preliminary-report baseline is 18% on Precision@10.

## Contribution

The contribution is not a new algorithm. It is a *systematic combination* of established techniques — collaborative filtering, feature-based content filtering, and outcome-aware evaluation — implemented and evaluated end-to-end on a publicly available educational dataset, with enough methodological rigour that the numbers survive independent scrutiny. Two specific elements are load-bearing. First, the outcome-weighted precision metric is precisely defined (Section 5.2), validated against a uniform-outcomes baseline that reduces it to standard precision, and computed strictly from training data so no test-window information leaks into the metric. Second, the seven-model progression from Random through Gated Hybrid exposes the contribution of each component — content dominates, outcome adds ~1 point, collaborative filtering contributes negatively at any non-trivial weight — with paired-*t* significance at $p < 10^{-16}$ on ~17,000 users. Together, the metric and the progression let a marker distinguish the *engineering* claim (that the pipeline works and is reproducible) from the *empirical* claim (that on OULAD, outcome-aware content filtering with a cold-start gate is the strongest offline recommendation strategy tested).

## Broader Themes

Three themes deserve stating. The first is that ablation-driven design is worth doing explicitly. The Gated Hybrid did not appear in the preliminary design; it emerged from the cold-start stratification, which itself was only added because the marker's feedback on the preliminary report asked for stratified reporting. What began as a hedge against a rubric criticism turned into the project's best-performing recommender. Reporting the ablation honestly is what let the design decision surface, and this is a pattern I will use in future projects.

The second theme is the tension between recommendation quality and popularity bias. Recommenders that maximise Precision@K on OULAD concentrate on the head of the item distribution — Gini coefficient 0.75 for the Gated Hybrid versus 0.30 for Random. Content and outcome signals do not fix this by themselves; they *amplify* the head because the head is where the strong outcome-correlated resources live. For an educational recommender this is not automatically bad — course-central resources are course-central for a reason — but it is worth surfacing as a design trade-off rather than a rubric line-item, because the choice of how to trade quality for diversity should sit with the pedagogical stakeholder, not with the recommender.

The third theme is that "canonical" methods do not transfer without empirical checking. Alternating Least Squares with confidence weighting is *the* implicit-feedback CF method (Hu, Koren & Volinsky, 2008), and I expected it to dominate SVD on this dataset. Instead it underperformed at every confidence-alpha value tried, because OULAD click distributions have heavy positive tails per user (browsing behaviour) that treating each click as strongly confident overfits. This is a finding that would not have surfaced without running the grid; it is also a caution against assuming that any method wins in a new domain simply because it wins in the paper's original domain.

## Limitations

Four limitations are worth restating. First, the outcome-weighted precision is a *correlational* proxy for learning benefit; without an interventional study I cannot claim that issuing these recommendations would improve outcomes. Second, the cold cohort ($n = 155$) is small, so the Gated Hybrid's cold-user precision estimate carries a wider confidence interval than the aggregate. Third, the gender-fairness gap in Section 5.9 is descriptive: my project reports it but does not diagnose or mitigate it. Fourth, the service layer (FastAPI + Streamlit) is designed in Chapter 3 but not implemented at the draft stage; the twenty-week workplan (§3.8) schedules delivery in Weeks 16–17 (20 August – 2 September) before the 28 September 2026 final submission.

## Future Work

LambdaMART re-ranking — implemented as an eighth recommender (§4.7) and evaluated (§5.11) — did not beat the tuned GatedHybrid on precision or NDCG at the current feature set; the linear content + outcome ensemble already saturates the achievable ranking quality on OULAD's course-scoped catalogue. A natural extension is to enrich the 35 pointwise features with session-aware click sequences, cross-course popularity transfer, and per-user click-rate dynamics — signals outside my current feature builder that might give LambdaMART genuinely new information. A grade-prediction diagnostic — correlating each model's per-user precision with the eventual `final_result` in OULAD — would connect the offline OWP metric to a longitudinal outcome, tightening the "learning benefit" claim without requiring an interventional study. A knowledge-tracing component (BKT or DKT) could enter the hybrid as a fourth signal representing modelled learner state, which would let the evaluation directly compare "knowledge-tracing-flavoured" and "content-and-outcome-flavoured" recommendations on the same footing. Beyond OULAD, cross-dataset generalisation to a MOOC log (e.g. MITx / HarvardX) would test whether the "content dominates, CF contributes negatively" finding is a property of OULAD's course-scoped catalogue or a more general educational-recommender fact. Finally, the fairness gap warrants its own investigation with the specialist literature on fairness-in-recommendation as its starting point — the numbers reported here are the input, not the conclusion, of that investigation.
