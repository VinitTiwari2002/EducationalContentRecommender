# Conclusion {#sec:conclusion}

## Summary

This project set out to build and rigorously evaluate a hybrid recommendation engine combining collaborative filtering, content matching, and learning-outcome signals on OULAD, delivered as a reproducible pipeline under the CM3005 template. Seven recommenders — Random, Popularity, SVD, ALS, Content, Hybrid, GatedHybrid — plus a LambdaMART two-stage reranker sit behind a common interface. The evaluation reports six metrics on a primary temporal split with 95% bootstrap CIs, five-fold temporal cross-validation, paired-*t* with Bonferroni adjustment, ablation, cold-start stratification, a fairness audit, four-axis hyperparameter sensitivity, and a three-seed robustness sweep. The GatedHybrid is the leading model on Precision@10, NDCG@10, and OWP@10; the lead survives CV. The relative gain over the preliminary-report baseline is 18% on Precision@10. A FastAPI service, Streamlit dashboard, and two-stage Docker image round out the delivery.

## Contribution

The contribution is not a new algorithm. It is a *systematic combination* of established techniques — collaborative filtering, content filtering, and outcome-aware evaluation — implemented and evaluated end-to-end on a public educational dataset with methodological rigour, plus a working serving stack and a LambdaMART reranker. Load-bearing pieces: (i) outcome-weighted Precision@K, precisely defined (§5.2), reducing to standard precision under a uniform-outcome null and computed strictly from training data; (ii) an eight-model progression exposing each component's contribution — content dominates, outcome adds ~1 P@10 point, CF contributes nothing above content+outcome — supported by paired-*t* with Bonferroni on ~17K per-user observations (§5.5, adjusted $p \le 5 \times 10^{-5}$ against Content and numerically zero against Random / Popularity / SVD / ALS on precision, NDCG, and OWP); (iii) the FastAPI + Streamlit + Docker stack that makes the recommendations *inspectable*, not just reported.

## Broader Themes

Three themes deserve stating. The first is that ablation-driven design is worth doing explicitly. The Gated Hybrid did not appear in the preliminary design; it emerged from the cold-start stratification, itself added only because marker feedback asked for stratified reporting. What began as a hedge against a rubric criticism turned into the project's best-performing recommender. Reporting the ablation honestly is what let the design decision surface — a pattern I will reuse.

The second theme is the tension between recommendation quality and popularity bias. Recommenders that maximise Precision@K on OULAD concentrate on the head of the item distribution — Gini 0.74 for the Gated Hybrid versus 0.32 for Random. Content-based cosine similarity drives this concentration; the outcome signal turns out to be essentially orthogonal to popularity (§5.8, $r \approx -0.08$), so it neither amplifies nor mitigates it. Head-concentration is not automatically bad for an educational recommender — course-central resources are course-central for a reason — but it is worth surfacing as a design trade-off: the choice of how to trade quality for diversity should sit with the pedagogical stakeholder, not with the recommender.

The third theme is that "canonical" methods do not transfer without empirical checking. Alternating Least Squares with confidence weighting is *the* implicit-feedback CF method (Hu, Koren & Volinsky, 2008), and I expected it to dominate SVD on this dataset. Instead it underperformed at every confidence-alpha value tried, because OULAD click distributions have heavy positive tails per user (browsing behaviour) that treating each click as strongly confident overfits. This is a finding that would not have surfaced without running the grid; it is also a caution against assuming that any method wins in a new domain simply because it wins in the paper's original domain.

## Limitations

Four limitations are worth restating. First, the outcome-weighted precision is a *correlational* proxy for learning benefit; without an interventional study I cannot claim that issuing these recommendations would improve outcomes. Second, the cold cohort ($n = 155$) is small, so the Gated Hybrid's cold-user precision estimate carries a wider confidence interval than the aggregate. Third, the gender-fairness gap in Section 5.9 is descriptive: this project reports it but does not diagnose or mitigate it, though the score decomposition surfaced by the FastAPI `/decompose` endpoint gives a natural entry point for a fairness-aware re-ranking step (see Future Work). Fourth, the outcome signal `mean_score_of_accessers` is itself observational: it captures the score of learners who *chose* to access an item, so it is vulnerable in principle to self-selection — high-outcome items could reflect the cohort that accessed them rather than any property of the item. Empirically the concern turned out to be small (§5.8 reports near-zero correlation between the outcome signal and item popularity, and the `hybrid_no_outcome` ablation shifts Gini by only −0.017), but a causal cleanse of the outcome signal would require instrumented data OULAD does not provide.

## Future Work

LambdaMART re-ranking — implemented as an eighth model (§4.6) and evaluated (§5.11) — did not beat the deployed GatedHybrid baseline on precision or NDCG at the current feature set; the linear content + outcome ensemble already saturates the achievable ranking quality on OULAD's course-scoped catalogue. A natural extension is to enrich the 35 pointwise features with session-aware click sequences, cross-course popularity transfer, and per-user click-rate dynamics — signals outside my current feature builder that might give LambdaMART genuinely new information. A **fairness-aware re-ranking layer** would insert a post-hoc penalty on the hybrid score that trades a controlled precision loss for a demographic-parity or exposure-parity improvement (Ekstrand et al., 2018; Zehlike et al., 2022); the score decomposition already returned by `/decompose` gives the mitigation step a direct hook into the deployed system. A grade-prediction diagnostic — correlating each model's per-user precision with the eventual `final_result` in OULAD — would connect the offline OWP metric to a longitudinal outcome, tightening the "learning benefit" claim without an interventional study. A knowledge-tracing component (BKT or DKT) could enter as a fourth signal representing modelled learner state, letting the evaluation directly compare "knowledge-tracing-flavoured" and "content-and-outcome-flavoured" recommendations on the same footing. Beyond OULAD, cross-dataset generalisation to a MOOC log (MITx / HarvardX) would test whether the "content dominates, CF contributes negatively" finding is a property of OULAD's course-scoped catalogue or a more general educational-recommender fact. Finally, the fairness gap warrants its own investigation with the specialist literature as its starting point — the numbers reported here are the input, not the conclusion, of that work.
